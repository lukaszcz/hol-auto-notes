 1. Introduction
In his paper, "The SUP-INF Method m Presburger ArithmeUc" [1, 2], Bledsoe
presents a method for proving certain theorems in a class of logical formulas that arise
frequently in program v e r i f i c a t i o n - P r e s b u r g e r formulas with universally quantified
variables. At the heart of the method are algorithms (SUP and INF) intended to
calculate the maximum and minimum values, respectively, that a real variable can take
subject to a set of linear constraints It is proved in that paper that the two algorithms
are conservative m the sense that SUP never computes a value too small and INF never
computes a value too large. That the two algorithms are conservative is sufficient to
guarantee that the SUP-INF method is consistent, i.e. that a formula determined by the
method to be valid is indeed valid. In this article it is shown that SUP and INF are not
only conservative, but exact in determining the maximum and minimum real values a
variable can take subject to a set of satisfiable constraints. This result gives rise to an
improved method of applying the SUP and INF algorithms that is able to determine
invalidity as well as validity, and that automatically finds counterexamples for formulas
determined to be invalid.
  The first few sections of this paper describe the original method and discuss its
completeness. The next sections introduce the improved method and describe its
relation to other methods and to linear programming. Implementation results are
described as well. The last sections give a proof of correctness of the SUP and | N F
algorithms, justifying the new method.

4. The Improved Method
As described in Section 1, the original method for testing whether or not a given set S
of inequalities (each of which contains at least one variable) has solutions is as follows:
A variable v occurring in S is chosen at random, and SUPs(v, O) and INFs(v, O) are
computed. If the closed interval [INFs(v, O), SUPs(v, 0 ) ] has no integer values, the
procedure halts, with the conclusion that S has no integer solutions. Otherwise, a new
variable is chosen, and the procedure is repeated. If all of the variables occurring in S
have been exhausted, the method fails. 3

domain than the origmal. Much more important, the new method provides a full
decision procedure over its domain of completeness rather than just a semidecision
procedure. Finally, and perhaps most important, the new method produces counterex-
amples for formulas that are found to be invahd.

The S U P - I N F approach may be viewed as transforming an anteger problem into the

real domain, solving it in that domain, and then interpreting the result back in the
integer domain. The success of the method derives at least in part from the fact that the

A large component of this expansion is associated with alternation of quantifiers, which
does not occur in the universal subclass For this reason, Cooper's method can be
expected to perform with greater efficiency over the subclass than it does over the full
class.

6. Implementatton Results
The improved SUP-INF method has been implemented in INTERLISPfor the PDP-10
computer and used extensively in proving verification conditions arising from JOVIAL
programs. The prover does not require any special initial form, and handles simple
examples in a second or less of C P U time. Most larger examples are solved many times
faster than a human could solve them. The counterexample feature has proved quite
useful in locating bugs in programs submitted to the verification process.
   We have found it efficient to implement the substitution of values by adding another
argument to SUP and INF which acts as an A-list retaining substituted values. The
functions U P P E R ( v ) and L O W E R ( v ) are modified to test whether v is bound on the A-
list and to return the bound value if it is.

7. Relation to Linear Programming
